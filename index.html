The challenge

An essential report that still runs on manual effort

The Eight O'Clock report is a formal end-of-day brief from department heads to the Commanding Officer. It covers personnel, material and training (PMT) status by department, and it is typically completed by department heads or their subordinates.

EFFORT

Significant manual work

Compiling the report takes time every day, pulling Sailors away from warfighting tasks.

ACCURACY

Error-prone

Manual collection and re-entry introduce mistakes into a report leaders rely on.

TRENDS

Limited visibility

Ships may not be aware of trends or what's being reported in systems of record.

ALIGNMENT

Cross-department blind spots

Departments report separately, so gaps and dependencies are easy to miss.

Much of this data already lives in authoritative systems of record. It can be aggregated and shared out to automate most of the process.

The solution

Two tools, one mission

SAGGLE ROC tackles two major problems: streamlining Eight O'Clock report generation, and accessing up-to-date information in bandwidth-limited environments.

PATH 1 · CONNECTED

Palantir Foundry

Integrates and visualizes PMT data from authoritative systems of record, built with the Eight O'Clock report in mind.

PATH 2 · BANDWIDTH-LIMITED

What if you can't access Foundry?

HTML files are sent to the ship and sit on a local computer. Small data packets then deliver current information from authoritative systems of record.

FLEETPULSE · PRODUCT TOUR

The low-bandwidth workflow

Judging criteria

How SAGGLE ROC delivers

Each criterion, and the specific ways this project answers it.

Warfighting & Deckplate Impact
How significantly does this offload watchstander cognitive burden or reclaim deckplate hours?
  • Automating data aggregation reclaims 17+ hours per week, or 912+ per ship each year
  • Time goes back to combat readiness, tactical training and maintenance
  • One consistent view reduces errors and shows trends and cross-department gaps
  • The CO gets a near-real-time readiness picture
Speed to Fleet
How quickly can this be fielded to operate at the edge under disconnected afloat (DDIL) conditions?
  • Built entirely using authorized tools
  • Small data packets travel by email, Teams or naval message traffic, with no new infrastructure
  • An HTML tool on a local computer needs no cloud connection
  • A second path means ships without Foundry access are still covered
  • Can be used today on an actual ship
Fleet Scalability
How widely can this solution scale across multiple ship classes, ratings, or operational domains?
  • Can be used seamlessly across other classes of ships
  • Spans 5 departments and 7 data sources in the MVP
  • A standardized report structure carries over to new ships and departments
  • Built to add more data sources and functionality
Barrier Removal
How effectively does this eliminate systemic bottlenecks, manual data entry, and legacy friction?
  • Removes manual double-entry from CSMP and RSUPPLY, and paper signatures
  • Auto-populates statuses such as fuel, CASREPs and food days remaining
  • Ends radio chatter to confirm whether GTGs are fully mission capable
  • Works where bandwidth or platform access is the barrier
Judges' Discretion
How compelling are the technical execution, Sailor user experience (UX), and overall innovation?
  • Dual-path design: Palantir Foundry for connected users, an HTML tool for bandwidth-limited ships
  • Delivers current data over channels ships already use
  • Realistic synthetic data modeled on real data sets and tailored by department
  • Built by technical experts paired with domain SMEs
